It tells you what just happened.

Your Mac does thousands of things a minute. MacXray turns the ones worth knowing into sentences, the moment they happen.

    You copied an image in Safari. It went to pboard in 2 formats.

  2. 09:41:07Clipboard

    Passwords copied something sensitive (probably a password). MacXray didn't read or save it.

  3. 09:40:52Processes

    You opened Xcode. It runs as 6 processes: the one you see and 5 helpers out of sight.

  4. 09:40:31Network

    Safari started talking to Apple.

  5. 09:39:58CPU cores

    Your 4 Performance cores just went to work (82% busy). Busiest: Xcode.

  6. 09:39:12Files

    Xcode changed 412 files in 10 seconds (caches).

  7. 09:38:40Memory

These are MacXray's own sentences. On your Mac, the names and numbers are yours.

Everything you copy, kept.

MacXray keeps what you copy, every format of every copy, in full. Go back to anything you copied and copy it again.

  • Nothing cut short. Long text, links, code, rich text and files, shown whole.
  • Images exactly as you copied them, at full size. Copy them again, save them or open them.

Questions

Good to know.

Is MacXray free?

Yes. Download it and use it on as many of your Macs as you like.

Will it slow my Mac down?

No. MacXray reads the counters macOS already keeps, the same ones Activity Monitor uses. It usually needs a few percent of one core and under 100 MB of memory, and it does the detailed work only for the view you have open.

Can it change anything on my Mac?

MacXray only watches. It doesn't change settings, delete files or quit apps. The one thing it changes is your clipboard, when you ask it to copy something again.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

Yes. MacXray is one app that runs natively on Apple silicon and on Intel Macs with macOS Sequoia or later. On an Intel Mac the CPU view shows a single kind of core, because Intel chips don't have Efficiency cores.

Why isn't it in the Mac App Store?

Apps from the Mac App Store run in a sandbox that keeps them from reading the system log and from watching files across the whole disk, which is much of what MacXray shows you. So it comes from here instead, signed and notarized by Apple, which checked it for malware.

Where is my clipboard history kept?

On your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/MacXray. In the Clipboard view you can delete single copies, or clear everything except the copies you pinned.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit MacXray and drag it from Applications to the Trash. To remove your clipboard history too, delete the MacXray folder in ~/Library/Application Support.

Is MacXray made by Apple?

No. MacXray is made by Victor Abuchi, an independent developer. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Apple.

Something isn't right. How do I tell you?

Open an issue on GitHub with what you saw, your Mac's model and its macOS version.
